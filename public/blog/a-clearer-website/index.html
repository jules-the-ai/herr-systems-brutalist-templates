Home/Blog/Web design

A clearer website starts with better questions.

Before colors, layouts, or clever effects, get clear on what your visitors actually need.

Start with the person visiting

A visitor rarely arrives thinking about your website’s design system. They have a task: find out what you do, check whether you can help, or work out how to contact you. Start there.

Write down the three questions a new customer asks most often. Those answers are a useful starting point for your page structure—and a better guide than a long list of features.

Start with what your customers need to do—not what you want your website to show off.

Give each page a job

A homepage introduces the business and points people in the right direction. A service page explains a specific offer. A contact page makes getting in touch feel straightforward. When every page has a purpose, deciding what belongs on it becomes easier.

Try finishing this sentence before drafting a page: “After reading this, the visitor should be able to…” If the answer is several unrelated things, consider splitting the content or making the main action clearer.

Make the important details easy to scan

Descriptive headings, short paragraphs, and specific link labels help readers find their way. You can still have personality without making someone decode what you mean.

Check the page on a phone. Does the headline explain the offer? Is the text comfortable to read? Can you find a next step without hunting? These ordinary details do more work than decorative complexity.

Choose a useful next step

Not everyone is ready to request a quote. Some people need a service explanation, an example, or a quick answer first. Match the next step to where the visitor is in that conversation.

A clear website is not necessarily a small website. It is one where the right information is easy to find, the choices make sense, and the visitor knows what happens next.
