KEEP THINGS RUNNING

Less dashboard. More clarity.

A hosting plan should explain more than storage and bandwidth. It should make clear how your domain connects, how changes reach the site, who handles updates, and what recovery looks like if something breaks. We shape the setup around your website rather than selling a complicated stack by default.

A small static website has different needs from an application with accounts and a database. We review the software, data, traffic expectations, and editing workflow before recommending a platform. Domain ownership and account access remain explicit, so a future move is practical rather than a negotiation.

Maintenance is a defined service, not an unlimited promise. We agree on update responsibilities, backup scope, retention, checks, and support availability. No provider can guarantee that a website will never fail; useful preparation means knowing what can be restored and how to report a problem.

01 // FROM QUESTION TO HANDOFF

A clear path through the work.

  1. 01

    Inspect the current setup

    Inventory the site, domain, email dependencies, existing accounts, and recovery needs. Identify anything that must remain untouched.

  2. 02

    Prepare and verify

    Configure the destination, test the site on a preview address, and verify the agreed backup or restore procedure before switching traffic.

  3. 03

    Switch and document

    Coordinate DNS or deployment changes, check important URLs, and hand over ownership records, renewal responsibilities, and support instructions.

02 // GOOD QUESTIONS

Before we get started.

Do you promise uninterrupted uptime?

No. Availability depends on providers, software, and external dependencies. Any service-level agreement, monitoring coverage, or response window must be specified in writing.

Will moving the website affect our email?

It can if DNS records are changed carelessly. We identify mail-related records and the email provider first; email migration is separate unless expressly included.
